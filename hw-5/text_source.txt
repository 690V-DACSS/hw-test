Boston is one of the oldest cities in the United States. The city is known for its history, 
neighborhoods, universities, and transportation system. Boston has many different 
communities, and each part of the city has its own social and economic patterns. 
Public transit plays an important role in daily life because many residents travel to work, 
school, hospitals, and government offices. Education is also central to Boston because 
the city is connected to colleges, research institutions, and public schools. 
People in Boston experience the city through housing, transit, health care, employment, 
and local government. These systems shape how neighborhoods develop and how resources 
are distributed across the city. Understanding urban patterns in Boston can help 
explain differences in opportunity, access, and public life.
